# QR table ordering lifecycle

Each restaurant table has a store-scoped record and a 256-bit random QR credential. PostgreSQL stores only its SHA-256 hash. The printed QR points to the customer web route; it grants access to that table's public menu and the ability to start a customer session, never to employee or administration APIs. Managers can disable QR ordering for a store or table, deactivate a table, rename it, or rotate its credential. Rotation immediately makes old QR links and sessions unusable. QR credentials are returned only during create/rotation so the manager can download or print them; an interrupted response is recovered by rotating again.

Opening the QR in the customer interface creates a separate 256-bit customer session credential stored as a hash. Each session is scoped to one store and table and expires four hours after it opens. Managers can close active sessions early. A session records the QR credential generation that created it, so a rotated code invalidates sessions from its prior generation. Disabling store ordering, disabling the table QR, deactivating the table, closing the session, or reaching its expiry blocks further orders from that session.

A customer session can submit multiple orders. Every submission creates its own `Order`, immutable price and recipe snapshots, order items, inventory reservations, station-grouped kitchen tickets, audit entry and outbox event in the existing serializable order transaction. Client totals are ignored. Current product, modifier, variant, manual-availability and ingredient stock rules are rechecked at acceptance. Idempotency keys make network retries return the same order without duplicating reservations or tickets. Financial obligations remain attached to individual orders; table sessions do not merge bills, payments or refunds.

Each order receives a distinct 256-bit tracking capability. Its database value is a hash; the raw capability is returned only to the ordering browser and is deterministic for the accepted session and idempotency key using `QR_TOKEN_SECRET`. That lets an exact retry recover the same tracking link without persisting its plaintext. The tracking endpoint reveals only the restaurant and table names, that order's item names and statuses, and its derived progress (`ACCEPTED`, `PREPARING`, `READY`, `SERVED`, or `CANCELLED`). It reveals no employee identity, payment data, customer contact data or other orders. The status page subscribes to a room for that single order and polls every 12 seconds if realtime is unavailable.

Kitchen state is authoritative. `PREPARING` appears after an item starts; `READY` appears when all non-cancelled items are ready or handed off; `SERVED` appears when all non-cancelled items are handed off. Existing staff cancellation and inventory-consumption rules continue to apply. QR customers cannot cancel accepted orders, access payment endpoints or reserve stock outside the standard order acceptance transaction.

Expired table-session rows are retained for audit and naturally stop authorizing at their expiry timestamp. A manager closes a session when the table is cleared; opening a later QR session creates a new session and never reuses or joins its previous orders. Existing orders remain individually payable through POS after a table session closes.
